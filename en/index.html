    PeopleLaw: an AI-based HR compliance platform that maps regulatory changes to company-specific actions and records the decisions behind them

    HR compliance platform | Aug 2026 to present | Builder: product definition, decision and approval criteria, agent development operations

  2. CASE STUDY 02 · AGENT OPERATIONS

    Agent operations: agents write the code, and only CI decides when the work is done

    How PeopleLaw was built | Aug to Oct 2026 | Claude Code, Codex, GitHub Actions

    AI agents wrote PeopleLaw's code. I set the scope and the approval criteria. Each session could write only to its own paths, a model from a different provider reviewed the work read-only, and tests and check tools were hash-locked so an agent could not edit them to pass. Work counted as done only when the CI stage gate passed. The first cross-review found 3 rule violations that the writing model's own review had missed.

  • AiDay: an AI agent that tells working parents what their child needs today

    Solo project | Apr to Jul 2026 | The idea I was selected for KAIST OverEdge with

    It interprets weather, air quality, pollen, and UV data against a child's profile and suggests what a parent may want to prepare that morning. I designed, built, and deployed it alone with Claude Code. Clothing advice is computed by rules, and only the summary report is written by Claude. It combines four public data feeds in real time and separates each user's data with row-level security. I surveyed 32 parents and interviewed parents of children under 8. Live service (Korean)

  • CareerPT: an AI coach that connects personal strengths to career direction and action

    KAIST MBA team project | Mar to Jun 2026 | Competitive analysis, AI feature implementation, evaluation harness

    Built on CliftonStrengths (the user's top 5 of 34 themes) and ICF coaching competencies, with a four-phase career interview. In a team project, I built a repeatable evaluation setup: 34 personas simulate coaching sessions to refine prompts, and the coach, the persona, and the judge run on different AI models to reduce the bias of a model grading its own answers. Action items pass a five-part validation gate before they are shown. We collected 4 user interviews, feedback from 11 users after launch, and 46 peer reviews. Live service (Korean)

  • Peace-Collector: an app for logging everyday anxiety and collecting small moments of calm

    KAIST MBA team project (four people) | Sep to Oct 2026 | Home and splash screens

    Four KAIST MBA students split the app by screen and feature and built it in parallel, each working with an AI coding agent. I owned the home screen and the splash screen: I wrote the mockups and screen specs first, then rebuilt the home prototype for the design refresh. We defined the user as women in their 20s to 40s who want short, low-pressure interactions, and shipped a mobile web beta that opens from a QR code with no install or sign-up. Anxiety logs stay on the device, and usage is measured only with consent (screen names and time spent, never the content). Built with Swift 6, SwiftUI, and WidgetKit for iOS, and React, TypeScript, Vite, and Tailwind CSS on Cloudflare Pages for the web beta. Web beta (Korean)

About

I worked in HR at a Korean construction company for 12 years and 4 months, covering training, payroll and compensation, recruiting, and HR planning in turn. Along the way I kept finding gaps in our systems and work processes and moving them into better systems. I joined the KAIST MBA program to study that work in more depth.

Two years of the MBA taught me that bringing in AI technology matters less than realigning work across departments around it and setting the standards for measuring results. When Korea introduced the 52-hour workweek, I drafted the changes to our pay and bonus structure and carried them through to the payroll system's calculation logic. In PeopleLaw, I designed AI to find the clauses a regulatory change might affect, while the HR manager decides whether to apply each change. I want to do this inside a company: decide how far a process should change when AI takes on part of it, and who checks the result.

My HR career is in my resume. This site covers only what I built and ran with AI. The full site is in Korean.
