One legal change never ends with one fix

01 · The problem in practice

When Korea introduced the 52-hour workweek, I drafted and simulated the changes to our pay and bonus structure. The law changed in one line, but the way working hours were entered, the payroll process, the payroll system's calculation logic, and the labor-management negotiation all had to change with it.

This repeats whenever a statute or court ruling changes. Yet many companies have no one dedicated to HR compliance, so an HR practitioner handles it alongside other duties, and the record of what was affected and why ends up scattered across one person's files and memory. When that person moves on, the next change starts the same review from scratch.

The gap existing solutions leave open

02 · The problem, seen again in the MBA

In the MBA I looked at this problem again from the market's side.

  • Global HR SaaS is strong at HR operations, but connecting a legal change to each company's own policies and forms, which differ by country, industry, and company, stays with the customer.
  • A custom build by a local systems integrator can fit one company, but it has to be reworked whenever the law or an internal document changes.
  • A general-purpose AI can answer questions about pasted policies, but updating relationships when a source changes, separating what has not been confirmed, and handing the basis to the next person are still left undone.

What was missing was a layer that connects external change to the work inside a company and keeps that company's own context.

Turning an HR practitioner's judgment into product principles

03 · What I decided to build

PeopleLaw handles one legal change in four stages: detect, company-specific impact mapping, review and decide, execute and retain. The design was driven by the risks I had seen in HR practice, not by the technology.

Legal safety lineThe system does not auto-confirm legal judgment. It routes the question to an expert as "needs review".
  • Impact mapping at the center: In practice, the effort went into finding which policy clause, form field, and HR system setting to change, not into reading the amendment.
  • AI drafts only: The wording of work rules becomes an employee's working conditions, so the practitioner confirms applicability and wording.
  • Legal judgment stays with people: In five areas, such as a disadvantageous change to work rules or the justification of dismissal and discipline, the system does not conclude. It routes the question to an expert as "needs review".
  • The basis is kept: Who decided what, when, and on what basis is kept per case, so the next change can start from that record even after the person in charge moves on.

Inside the product, and in building it

04 · How I used AI

Inside the product, AI is given only clause candidates, before-and-after comparisons, and draft wording. A rule engine checks conditions and computes deadlines from reference values approved by a person, and the server checks the AI's citations and numbers against the official statute text. Anything that cannot be confirmed in the source stays "unconfirmed".

In building it, AI agents wrote the code. I decided what to build, the lines AI must not cross, and the approval criteria. The work was split across lead, core, and UI sessions; a model from a different provider than the one that wrote the code reviewed it read-only; and only changes that passed the required tests were merged. Core data structures and reference values for judgment cannot change without my approval record, and a change without one fails the automated checks. Each design decision is written down in its own document.

What the cross-review found

In the first cross-review, the other model found that confirming a clause's impact and approving its wording shared a single status value. A practitioner who only confirmed the impact could have let AI-drafted wording enter an internal notice as if it were confirmed. In HR practice these are two different approvals, so I separated them and added a record of who confirmed the wording and when.
